# Effects of a Robot's Courageous Action<br>on Observers' Courage

---

## Problem: Valuable Actions Can Still Be Hard

- Speaking in front of others
- Speaking to an unfamiliar person about their behavior
- Asking someone for help

These actions do not involve strong physical danger.  
Still, they involve **social and psychological risks**, such as negative evaluation, rejection, and embarrassment.

This study focuses on **courage** that is needed when such risks make action difficult.

<div class="accent">
Definition of courage: trying to move toward a valuable goal even when one feels fear or risk.<br>
<span class="cite">[Rachman 1984; Woodard &amp; Pury 2007]</span>
</div>

---

## Observational Learning May Support Courage

- Observing another person face difficulty can affect the observer's behavior and self-efficacy  
  [Bandura 1977; Schunk &amp; Hanson 1989]
- Past studies have mainly examined task performance, learning outcomes, and self-efficacy after observation

<div class="accent">
It is still unclear whether courage involving fear or risk can change through observing another agent.
</div>

<br>

**=> Why has courage been difficult to study as a target of observational learning?**

---

## Courage Is Hard to Observe Because It Depends on Inner States

<div class="accent">
Definition of courage: trying to move toward a valuable goal even when one feels fear or risk.<br>
<span class="cite">[Rachman 1984; Woodard &amp; Pury 2007]</span>
</div>

- Courage is not determined only by the objective difficulty of the action
- Fear and hesitation before action are also important
- In human models, another person's inner state cannot be directly observed
- To study courage through observation, the inner state before action must be shown to the observer

---

## A Robot Can Show Inner States in a Controlled Way

<div class="cols">
<div class="box">

### Limits of Human Models

- Inner states are not directly visible
- Facial expression, voice, and silence can change across conditions
- It is hard to reproduce the same level of conflict

</div>
<div class="box">

### Advantages of Robots

- Inner processes can be externalized
- The presence and content of expressions can be controlled
- The process before action can be presented in a controlled way

</div>
</div>

<div class="accent accent-lower">
With a robot, inner states can be treated as something visible and experimentally controllable.
</div>

---

## Thought Bubbles Can Convey a Robot's Inner State

This study shows inner processes before action by thought bubbles and motion using projection mapping.

<div class="cols-bubble">
<div>

- A thought bubble near a robot's head can help observers understand where the robot is paying attention
- This suggests that a thought bubble can convey a state that is hard to see from the robot's outer behavior

</div>
<div>

<img src="image/thought_bubble_single.png" class="slide-img">
<div class="img-credit">[Nitada et al. 2021]</div>

</div>
</div>

<div class="accent accent-lower">
This study uses thought bubbles to present the robot's inner process before action.
</div>

---

## Inner Conflict as a Cue for Courage

This study focuses on **approach-avoidance conflict** as the robot's inner state before action.  
Approach-avoidance conflict is a state in which approach motivation and avoidance motivation occur at the same time. [Lewin 1931; Miller 1944]

<div class="cols">
<div class="box">

### Approach Motivation

- Moving toward a valuable goal

</div>
<div class="box">

### Avoidance Motivation

- Avoiding risk or negative outcomes

</div>
</div>

<div class="accent accent-lower">
Courage is related to moving toward a valuable action while feeling fear or risk. Therefore, showing the conflict between approach and avoidance may help observers understand fear and hesitation before action.
</div>

---

## Purpose of This Study

<div class="purpose-box">
This study examined whether observing a robot that visualizes approach-avoidance conflict affects observers' own courage.
</div>

<div class="note-box">
<strong>Additional point</strong><br>
This effect may differ depending on the observer's prior courage tendency.<br>
The effect of model observation can change depending on whether the observer sees the model as similar to the self. [Bandura 1977; Schunk &amp; Hanson 1989; Lucas et al. 2006]
</div><br>
=> Therefore, this study also considered differences in observers' prior courage tendency.

## Study 2: Purpose and Conditions

<div class="purpose-box">
<strong>Purpose</strong>: To examine how observing a robot that shows approach-avoidance conflict and then acts affects observers' own courage.
</div>

<div class="condition-grid">
<div class="box condition-card">

### 3-Factor Design

<p><strong>Between-participants factor</strong>: prior courage tendency group (low / high)</p>
<p><strong>Within-participants factors</strong>: conflict x action</p>

<table class="cond-table">
<thead>
<tr><th></th><th>Conflict</th><th>Action</th></tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td>1</td><td>No conflict</td><td>No action</td></tr>
<tr><td>2</td><td>No conflict</td><td>Action</td></tr>
<tr><td>3</td><td>Conflict</td><td>No action</td></tr>
<tr><td>4</td><td>Conflict</td><td>Action</td></tr>
</tbody>
</table>

</div>
<div class="box condition-card">

### Meaning of the Factors

- <strong>No conflict</strong>: only one motivation related to action was shown
- <strong>Conflict</strong>: approach and avoidance motivations were shown together
- <strong>No action</strong>: the robot did not make a warning statement
- <strong>Action</strong>: the robot made a warning statement

</div>
</div>

<div class="accent condition-note">
<strong>Why action was manipulated</strong>: Study 2 examined whether the effect of conflict expression appears only when the robot actually acts, or whether showing conflict before action can also affect observers.
</div>

## Study 2: Hypothesis and Dependent Variable

<div class="panel-grid measure-grid">
<div class="box">

### Hypothesis

<p>Observing the robot that shows conflict and then gives a warning will increase observers' own courage more than the other conditions.</p>

</div>
<div class="box">

### Dependent Variable

<p><strong>Courage scale</strong><br>Self-rating of the observer's own courage.</p>

</div>
</div>

<!-- _class: study2-result -->

## Study 2: Results

<div class="study2-result-main">
<div class="study2-result-copy">
<p class="analysis-tag"><strong>Analysis</strong>: three-factor ANOVA (B-W-W)</p>
<p class="result-summary">For observers' own courage, the interaction between prior courage group and conflict was significant.</p>
<ul>
<li>Interaction<br><strong>F(1, 124) = 7.513, p = .007</strong></li>
<li><strong>Low group</strong>: courage tended to be higher in the conflict condition (p = .052)</li>
<li><strong>High group</strong>: courage was significantly lower in the conflict condition (p = .038)</li>
</ul>
</div>
<figure class="study2-result-figure">
<img src="image/study2_courage_simple_effects.png" alt="Simple effects of conflict by prior courage tendency group in Study 2">
</figure>
</div>

<div class="study2-takeaways">
<ul>
<li>The effect of conflict expression on observers' own courage <strong>differed by prior courage tendency</strong>.</li>
<li><strong>Action</strong> did not show a significant effect on observers' own courage.</li>
<li><strong>Hypothesis</strong>: the conflict + action condition will increase courage the most.<br>=> Not supported.</li>
</ul>
</div>

---

<!-- _class: study2-interpretation -->

## Study 2: Discussion

<ul class="interpretation-list">
<li><strong>Low prior courage group</strong>: The robot that tried to move forward while feeling conflict may have been seen as a model similar to the self. This may have supported the feeling that "I may be able to act even with anxiety or hesitation."</li>
<li><strong>High prior courage group</strong>: Conflict expression may have been seen as hesitation or lack of confidence. This may have lowered observers' own courage.</li>
<li><strong>Implication</strong>: The same conflict expression can work in opposite directions depending on the observer's prior courage tendency.</li>
</ul>

## Overall Conclusion: The Effect of Conflict Expression Differs by Observer

Conflict expression was perceived as conflict and made the robot's action look more courageous.

### Study 2

Observing conflict expression can affect observers' own courage. However, the effect differed by prior courage tendency.

For robot expressions that support courage, it is important not only to show action, but also to decide how to visualize approach-avoidance conflict before action and to whom it should be shown.

## Limitations and Future Work

- This study did not measure whether observers actually choose a courageous action

- Future work should measure psychological processes such as self-similarity, empathy, and self-efficacy to explain why the effect differed between groups

- The stimulus scene and expression method were limited, so the effect should be tested in other courage-related scenes and with other robot expressions

## Future Research: From Self-Rated Courage to Actual Action

<div class="purpose-box">
This study showed that conflict expression can affect observers' own courage. The next question is whether this effect leads to actual interpersonal action.
</div>

<div class="panel-grid">
<div class="box">

### Idea 1: Speaking in a Meeting

- A robot joins a discussion with several people
- The robot shows conflict and then disagrees or points out a problem
- We examine whether participants speak more or point out more problems afterward

</div>
<div class="box">

### Idea 2: Asking or Pointing Out in a Presentation

- A human presenter gives a presentation
- The robot shows conflict and then asks a question or points out a problem
- We examine whether participants tell the presenter another question or concern afterward
